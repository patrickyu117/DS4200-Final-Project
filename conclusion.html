What Can We Conclude:

The visualizations and analyses we conducted with this dataset collectively show how global economic and educational factors impact life expectancy. Generally, higher education and GDP per capita lead to a higher life expectancy and lower HIV/AIDS rate. Here were some of our key findings.

Key Findings:

  1. Over time from 2000-2015, life expectancy overall has increased across the world. Countries that have consistently exhibited higher expectancies also have higher GDPs per capita, which can be associated with better healthcare and higher standards of living.
  2. Similar to GDP, countries that are classified as "Developed" nations consistently have higher life expectancies than "Developing" nations. GDP per capita has a slight positive correlation with life expectancy, but countries that have disproportionately GDP to population ratios may be skewing data.
  3. The number of years in education are positively correlated with life expectancy. Even countries with lower GDP but have on average more years of school exhibit higher life expectancies.
  4. Countries with higher average years of schooling have lower rates of HIV/AIDS, showing that education plays a big part in reducing the spread of infectious diseases through greater health awareness, preventative behaviors, and access to care.
  5. Countries with lower rates of HIV/AIDS have a much higher average life expectancy than countries with higher rates.

Potential Future Work:

  • Obtain a dataset from 2015-2024 to see if the trends we noticed continue and if life expectancy continues to rise.
  • Analyze trends over time to see how life expectancy has changed in response to global events, such as pandemics or economic crises.
  • Include more variables in the analysis, such as healthcare spending, access to clean water, and nutrition, to see how they impact life expectancy.
  • Use machine learning techniques to predict life expectancy based on various factors, such as GDP, education, and healthcare access.
  • Conduct a more in-depth analysis of specific countries or regions to understand the unique factors that contribute to life expectancy in those areas.
