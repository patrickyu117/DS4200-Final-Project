What Can We Conclude:

The visualizations and analyses we conducted with this dataset collectively show how global economic and educational factors impact life expectancy. Countries that are developed, have higher GDPs and longer schooling durations consistently demonstrate higher life expectancies. This correlation suggests that countries that are able to invest in health education and successful healthcare overall beneficially impact their citizens' life expectancies. However, outliers and exceptions indicate that factors such as natural disasters, healthcare access, and social determinants also play crucial roles in estimating life expectancy. Overall, the findings show that life expectancy is highly dependent on a country's economic and educational advancements towards healthcare, but may have other underlying factors.
