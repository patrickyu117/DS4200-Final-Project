What Can We Conclude:

The visualizations and analyses we conducted with this dataset collectively show how global economic and educational factors impact life expectancy. Overall, we've discovered that factors such as a country's GDP, status, education level, etc. are correlated with its population's life expectancy. Here were some of our key findings.

Key Findings:
  • Over time from 2000-2015, life expectancy overall has increased across the world. Countries that have consistently exhibited higher expectancies also have higher GDPs per capita, which can be associated with better healthcare and higher standards of living.
  • Similar to GDP, countries that are classified as "Developed" nations consistently have higher life expectancies than "Developing" nations. GDP per capita has a slight positive correlation with life expectancy, but countries that have disproportionately GDP to population ratios may be skewing data.
  • The number of years in education are positively correlated with life expectancy. Even countries with lower GDP but have on average more years of school exhibit higher life expectancies.
